Six jobs, start to finish. What the caller described, what we actually found, what it took to put right, and what it cost.

6 files

01 Residential

Row home, South Philadelphia · Leak Detection

A ceiling stain that was not the roof

What they told us
Brown ring on the bedroom ceiling, growing slowly. Two roofers had already been out and found nothing wrong up top.
What we found
Thermal showed a cooler band running away from the stain toward the bathroom wall, not toward the roof. The supply line to the shower valve had a pinhole leak soaking the joist bay and tracking sideways before it showed.
What we did
Opened a single section of ceiling directly over the valve, replaced the failed run, dried the cavity to meter, then closed and finished it.
How it ended
One opening instead of a roof replacement. Ceiling repainted the same week.

02 Residential

Twin, Delaware County · French Drains & Sump

A basement that flooded every heavy rain

What they told us
Two inches of water across the basement floor after any real storm. A pump had been fitted by someone else and it was not keeping up.
What we found
The ground sloped back toward the house on the rear elevation and both downspouts discharged within a metre of the wall. The existing pump was undersized and discharging into the same saturated ground it was pumping out of.
What we did
Regraded the rear, took both downspouts to a buried line and out to the street side, cut an interior perimeter drain to a new pit, and fitted a correctly sized pump with a battery backup.
How it ended
Dry through the following winter, including the storm that put water into two neighbouring houses.

03 Commercial

Apartment building, Greater Philadelphia · Mold Remediation

Recurring mold in three units of one stack

What they told us
Three tenants in a vertical stack reporting smell and visible growth. It had been cleaned twice already and came back both times.
What we found
A shared bathroom vent riser was terminating in the roof void instead of outside. Warm wet air was condensing in the void and running back down the stack, wetting the top of each unit's ceiling.
What we did
Contained and remediated all three units under negative air, then fixed the cause: terminated the riser correctly through the roof, insulated the run, and tested air quality after the rebuild.
How it ended
Clearance passed on all three. No recurrence reported since.

04 Residential

Row home, North Philadelphia · Exterior Repairs

A front elevation that looked fine from the pavement

What they told us
Buying the house. The inspector flagged damp on an upstairs internal wall and recommended a moisture opinion before closing.
What we found
Pointing across the front envelope had failed and the caulking around every window and door perimeter had perished. Wind driven rain was going through the joints and into the cavity, then showing up on the inside face two rooms away from the window it entered.
What we did
Repointed the failed courses across the elevation, cut out and replaced the perimeter sealant on every opening, and re-checked the internal wall with meters after the first heavy rain.
How it ended
Findings used in the sale negotiation before the work was booked.

05 Commercial

Managed property, Philadelphia · Water Damage Restoration

A common room floor lifting with no visible water

PhotographFinding
What they told us
Facilities reported the common room floor buckling. No standing water anywhere and nobody could say how long it had been happening.
What we found
A slow fitting leak had been wetting the subfloor for months. The finish floor had been trapping it, so the moisture had spread well past anything visible from the surface and the framing below had started to go.
What we did
Lifted the affected area, extracted and dried the substrate to a documented standard, replaced the framing that could not be saved, and produced an annotated photo report the managing agent used for their insurance claim.
How it ended
Claim accepted on the documentation. Room back in use in under two weeks.

The pattern

The cheap job and the expensive one start identically.

Every file on this page began as a small complaint. A stain, a smell, a bit of damp after rain. The difference between the ones that cost a few hundred and the ones that cost tens of thousands was almost never the severity at the start. It was how long it ran before anyone looked properly.

That is the whole argument for an assessment. Not because something is definitely wrong, but because finding out is the only cheap part.
