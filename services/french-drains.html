Drainage Solutions & French Drains

A basement that floods every spring is not a basement problem. It is a drainage problem that shows up in the basement.

Discuss this project 1-833-466-4788

What this actually is

A French drain is a gravel-packed trench with a perforated pipe in it, set below the level you are trying to keep dry, that gives groundwater somewhere easier to go than through your wall.

The hard part is not the trench. It is working out where the water is actually coming from, where it can legally and practically be discharged to, and what grade the line needs to hold over its whole run. Get that wrong and you have buried an expensive pipe that does nothing.

We survey the lot, establish the flow, and design the run before anyone puts a shovel in the ground. Depth, fall, outlet and filtration. On most Philadelphia row properties this is combined with regrading, downspout extension and a sump discharge line.

Signs you have this problem

  • Water across the basement floor after heavy rain, then gone by Tuesday
  • Standing puddles against the foundation wall hours after it stops raining
  • Soil that has settled into a dish around the building
  • A window well that fills up
  • Efflorescence, the white chalky residue, along the bottom of the foundation
  • Downspouts discharging within a few feet of the wall

How the work runs

Every job in this category follows the same order.

  • 01

    Site and water survey

    Where the water arrives, where it pools, where it can be taken to. Elevations shot across the whole run.

  • 02

    Design the system

    Depth, fall, pipe size, filter fabric and outlet. Combined with regrading and downspout work where the lot allows it.

  • 03

    Excavate and install

    Trench, bed, perforated line, washed stone and fabric. Utilities located and protected before we dig.

  • 04

    Discharge and restore

    Outlet to daylight or to a pumped discharge, then backfill, regrade and restore the surface.

  • 05

    Water test

    We run water through the finished system and show you it moving before we leave.

Questions people ask

Drainage & French Drains, answered plainly.

Exterior drainage stops water before it reaches the wall, which is always the better outcome, but it needs room to excavate and somewhere legal to discharge. Interior drainage with a sump manages water that has already arrived. On a tight city lot the answer is often both, and the assessment is what tells us which.

Yes. An exterior line is an excavation. We photograph everything first, protect what can be protected, and restore the surface as part of the scope. We will tell you honestly at the assessment what the yard will look like afterward.

A typical residential run is three to five working days, weather permitting. Longer runs, difficult access and utility conflicts extend that, and we tell you which of those apply before you sign anything.
